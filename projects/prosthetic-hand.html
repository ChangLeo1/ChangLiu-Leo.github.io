THE CHALLENGE

From muscle signal
to meaningful action.

Conventional myoelectric prosthetic hands can be limited by low dexterity, rigid control strategies and weak sensory feedback.

This project was designed as a low-cost, real-time human–machine system. Surface electromyography captures motion intent, embedded processing classifies gestures, servo actuation executes the command, and fingertip sensing returns information about the physical interaction.

ENGINEERING LAYERS

A

AI & SIGNAL PROCESSING

sEMG → 24-D features → PNN

Eight sEMG channels are segmented using a moving-average and threshold approach. Three time-domain features—MAV, RMS and WL—form a 24-dimensional vector for the PNN classifier.

B

EMBEDDED CONTROL

STM32F1 + PCA9685 + servos

The STM32F1 is the control core. The PCA9685 provides multi-channel PWM output for servo positioning while sensor data is sampled through the microcontroller interface.

STM32
C

MULTISENSOR FEEDBACK

Pressure · tactile · temperature

FSR402 sensors measure grip force, PVDF film detects touch and slip, and HDC1080 provides temperature sensing. Feedback is translated into vibration and temperature cues.

FPT

MECHANICAL DESIGN

Designed around
human-scale constraints.

The proposal specifies a six-degree-of-freedom hand: two degrees of freedom at the thumb and one for each remaining finger. Tendon-driven servo actuation and return springs support finger motion.

TEMPERATURE FEEDBACK

Closed-loop thermal response

The system uses a Peltier device with PID control. At 27 °C room temperature, the proposal reports 7 s response at 25 °C and 31 °C targets, with longer response times for larger temperature differences.
